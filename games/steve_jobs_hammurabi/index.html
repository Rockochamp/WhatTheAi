What is Steve Jobs – Hammurabi?

Steve Jobs – Hammurabi is a free turn-based resource management game. Each year, choose how much land to buy or sell, how many acres to plant, and how well to feed your population. Your aim is to finish a ten-year reign with as many people as possible while surviving shortages and random events. All three editions share the same economy. The Claude Fable 5.2 edition fixes each reign’s luck in advance, so a built-in scribe can rule the same fate for comparison and every player can attempt the same daily fate.

Choose a version

Each one opens that edition directly. These describe the implementations in this collection; they are not rankings of the underlying AI models.

  1. Claude Fable 5.2

    The kingdom as a living mosaic, sliders with a live grain ledger, and a scribe who advises three times. Luck is fixed per reign, so you see how the scribe would have ruled the same fate, and today’s fate is shared by every player.

  2. GPT-6 Astra

    A responsive budget, annual reports, chronicle and a living 3D kingdom. A panorama fallback keeps the strategy interface available without WebGL.

  3. Grok 3 Reasoning

    The original form-based strategy version, with annual decisions, resource totals, help and local/global rankings.

How to play

  1. Enter a ruler name if you want, then start a reign. In the Claude edition, choose today’s shared fate or a new one.
  2. Set annual land, planting and feeding decisions. The Claude edition uses sliders with a live ledger; the Astra and Grok editions use number fields. Buying and selling land cannot happen in the same year.
  3. Confirm the year and read the report before making the next decisions. A reign ends after ten years or when population reaches zero.

A few useful tips

One person can farm ten acres; planting also costs food for seed. The starting granary cannot feed a hundred people for long, so land comes first.

Hunger builds when rations fall below twenty bushels a person and only kills past a breaking point. A lean year is survivable; several in a row are not.

In the Claude edition the scribe advises three times per reign, and the ranking places unaided reigns above aided ones with the same score.
