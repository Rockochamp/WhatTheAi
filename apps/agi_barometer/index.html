What is AGI Probability Barometer?

The AGI Probability Barometer is an experimental visualization of a hiring-based formula. Its name is historical: the displayed percentages are not validated probabilities of artificial general intelligence. The idea being tested is that a lab close to AGI would advertise fewer jobs. The Claude Fable 5.2 and Grokbot editions use sourced counts of open listings; the Claude edition can also take a live reading for three labs. The two Gemini editions use fixed example numbers stored in their source code.

Choose a version

Each one opens that edition directly. These describe the implementations in this collection; they are not rankings of the underlying AI models.

  1. Claude Fable 5.2

    A brass barometer you can tap. The needle shows how far five labs’ job listings sit below their peak; tapping the glass takes a live reading from three labs’ public job boards, and a second hand marks the recorded reading for comparison.

  2. Grokbot Edition

    Live job-listing counts with source links and dates, one hiring-drop index formula, a dashed live-count segment on the chart and a no-data state for labs without a verified count.

  3. Gemini 3.5 Flash (High)

    Three stored quarterly inputs per lab, a combined indicator, trend chart and interactive formula details.

  4. Gemini 3.1 Pro (High)

    The earlier two-quarter dashboard, with one gauge per lab and a simpler decline-based formula.

How to use it

  1. In the Claude edition, read the dial and the table, then tap the glass for a live reading. The brass hand keeps marking the recorded reading so you can see which way the needle moved.
  2. Open the Grokbot or Gemini editions to inspect their lab gauges and the stored inputs. The Flash edition adds a combined gauge, a trend chart and interactive lab details.
  3. Read the methodology below before interpreting any percentage. The editions use different time windows and formulas.

Before you begin

A fall in job listings could reflect budgets, recruiting cycles, restructuring or many other causes. It does not demonstrate AGI. Listings are adverts, not hires.

A live reading in the Claude edition contacts the labs’ public job boards directly from your browser and is stored only there. If a board does not answer, the recorded count stays.

The earlier quarterly values in every edition have no source citations. Treat them as unverified example inputs, not hiring statistics.

Methodology and limitations

The Grokbot Edition uses one formula for every lab and for the combined total: max(0, 1 − latest / peak), called the hiring-drop index. It loads live listing counts from listings.json, each with a source URL and retrieval time. Labs without a verified latest count show “no data” and are left out of the combined index. Earlier Q1–Q3 points come from the older editions and remain unverified.

The Claude Fable 5.2 edition uses the same per-lab formula and the same recorded counts. Its dial shows one minus the labs’ latest counts added together over their highest counts added together. Tapping the glass asks the public job-board feeds of OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI for today’s totals from your browser; Google DeepMind and Meta have no feed a browser can read, so they keep their recorded counts. Live readings are stored only in your browser.

The Gemini 3.1 Pro edition calculates max(0, 100 × (1 − Q2 / Q1)) for each lab. The Flash edition uses max(0, 100 × (1 − Q3 / max(Q1, Q2, Q3))). Its combined gauge applies the same decline-from-peak idea to the quarterly totals.

These formulas express a percentage decline in the stored inputs. They are not calibrated against AGI outcomes, do not establish causation and do not estimate a reliable arrival date.

The Flash screen originally labelled a June 22, 2026 cutoff while including Q3 2026 values. That timing is inconsistent with a completed Q3 observation. The values remain unchanged for reproducibility; their provenance and timing are unverified.
